# Stairs join neighbouring levels through the round space inside the lower ring

Two neighbouring levels are joined by stairs with a model, not by the invisible shaft. The owner wants this route: a passenger comes down from the ring, turns to the platform, and after one more turn has the stairs down ahead. So the stairs start in the corridor wall that faces away from the centre, and the first flight leads back towards the centre, between the wall of the hall and the stairs from the ring. The two first flights of one platform side meet on a landing, and one second flight crosses below the hall to a shared platform in the middle of the ring of the lower level. Two exit stairs lead from there to the ring.

The stairs touch the lower level only at the empty round space inside its ring. The ring gallery around it has no wall or railing on its inner edge, so the models fit every angle between the two levels and no part of the lower hall has to be cut. The landing lies above the roof of the lower ring for the same reason. A first design led away from the centre and came back through a long corridor. It was dropped because the walk was twice as long and the route did not match the owner's idea.

The owner wants the exit stairs to point to the two exits of the lower ring. The second flights always arrive along the axis across the upper hall, so for many angles the exits lie below them, most of all when the two levels are parallel. The script therefore turns the exit stairs to the exits only when that direction lies between the two second flights, which holds for levels that cross at about a right angle. Otherwise the exit stairs point along the upper hall. Exit stairs below a second flight would have no headroom next to the shared platform.

The openings in the upper hall are real: the mod ships a copy of the hall of the game without four corridor walls, built by a tool from the game files. Every level uses the copy, because it also carries ceiling lights that are on all day. The cost is that the copy does not follow later changes of the game model until the tool runs again. A level below a gap keeps its shaft, because stairs across 36 m would need their own design.
